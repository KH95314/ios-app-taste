dedication), so they can be copied, changed and shipped with no condition and no credit. The credit
is kept here anyway, with where each was found.

They are stand-ins. An app made with this skill shows its own product's pictures, not these.

## How each licence was checked

- **StockSnap.** Each photo's page links "CC0" to creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0, and
  https://stocksnap.io/license says every image on the site is under CC0.
- **Flickr.** The photo's page gives its licence as "CC0 (Public Domain Dedication)".
- **The Met.** Each object's record in the museum's open collection says it is in the public
  domain, and https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources says images of such works are
  free for unrestricted use under CC0.

A licence on a page is the publisher's statement. Nobody here checked that each uploader had the
right to give the picture away. If you ship one of these in a product, look at its source page
yourself.

## What is not here, on purpose

- No photograph of a person whose face can be recognised. A licence covers the photograph, not the
  person in it.
- No real company's logo.
- Nothing under a licence that asks for credit or forbids changes.
